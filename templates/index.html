Background

In SMA Pradita Dirgantara, students and citizens mostly eat from rukan. But most of the time, the kitchen won't use every single ingredient they have, hence waste from ingredients begins to pile up, creating a problem needing to be solved.

Contents of a Dragon Fruit

The Dragon fruit (Hylocereus spp.) has lots of nutritious and beneficial components that are rich in various bioactive phytochemical compounds:

Phenolic Acids & Flavonoids

Active antioxidants helping eliminate free radicals and protect cellular structures.

Antioxidant

Betalains

Natural red-violet pigments responsible for vibrant color and active anti-inflammatory activity.

Anti-inflammatory

Anthocyanins

Water-soluble pigments with demonstrated antimicrobial properties.

Anti-microbial

Interdisciplinary STEM Framework

Integrating Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics for sustainable food innovation:

Science
  • Bioactive Compounds Dragon fruit (both flesh and skin) provides antioxidants, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial agents such as flavonoids, phenolic acids, betalains, and anthocyanins.
  • Sustainability & Nutrition Utilizing the skin alongside the flesh minimizes biological waste while maximizing dietary fiber and nutritional value.
  • Gel Formation Gelatin molecules cross-link upon cooling to form a semi-solid gel matrix, creating the classic chewy texture.
  • Solute & pH Dynamics Dissolved sugars act as natural preservatives and flavor enhancers, while citric acid adjusts the pH to balance flavor and ensure product stability.
Technology
  • Interactive Website Develops an interactive website to display product ingredients, educational material, and targeted marketing/promotional content.
Engineering
  • Heat Transfer Thermal energy moves from the stove to the mixture during the cooking phase.
  • Rheology & Viscosity Mechanical blending creates a uniform puree, with viscosity increasing as the mixture thickens and water evaporates.
  • Phase Change Controlled cooling transitions the solution from a liquid state into a cohesive semi-solid gel.
Mathematics
  • Ratio Calculations Precise measurement ratios of sugar, citric acid, and gelatin relative to fruit mass to ensure optimal gelling, taste balance, and shelf-life prediction.

Table 3 — Step-by-Step Methodology

1

Preparation

Thoroughly wash the dragon fruit. Separate the pink skin and the red flesh, ensuring a 1:1 ratio by weight. Clean the outer skin of any rough scales or blemishes.

⤵
2

Steaming the Skin

Place the dragon fruit skin into the steamer. Steam until the skin is softened. This helps reduce bitterness and soften the tough outer layer.

⤵
3

Blending

Transfer the softened skin and fresh dragon fruit flesh into the blender. Blend until completely smooth and uniform.

⤵
4

Combining Ingredients

Pour the blended mixture into the pot. Add 100g gelatin, 380g sugar, ½ teaspoon cornstarch, and ½ teaspoon citric acid. Mix thoroughly using a spoon or spatula.

⤵
5

Cooking and Dissolving

Heat over low-to-medium heat. Stir continuously to prevent burning and ensure the gelatin and sugar fully dissolve into a smooth, thick liquid.

⤵
6

Molding

Pour the hot mixture evenly into a rectangular metal mold or any preferred container.

⤵
7

Setting

Let it cool at room temperature, then refrigerate until completely firm and set.

⤵
8

Cutting and Finishing

Remove from the mold and place on a clean surface. Use a knife to cut into neat cubes or rectangular pieces.
